Research standards

Methods before
marketing claims.

These are the principles the project intends to follow. They are commitments for future work, not a claim of accreditation or completed testing.

1. Document the test conditions

Record the exact device model and variant, software/build, workload and tool versions, settings, duration, network state, ambient conditions where measured, and any deviations. Avoid vague labels such as “maximum performance” without defining the settings.

2. Repeat tests where practical

Use repeated runs for variable workloads. Retain valid run-level data and report a suitable summary such as mean and range or median. Explain exclusions rather than silently discarding inconvenient results.

3. Distinguish measurements from impressions

Benchmark scores, observed battery percentage changes, measured surface temperatures and subjective usability are different evidence types. Label each correctly. Battery percentage is not a direct measurement of watt-hours or battery capacity.

4. Use fair comparisons

Compare results only when workloads, versions, settings and conditions are sufficiently comparable. Disclose differences that could affect interpretation. Do not turn a single run into a universal claim.

5. State limitations

Identify equipment limits, missing readings, uncontrolled conditions, possible measurement error and questions the study cannot answer. Do not infer causation from correlation or one observation.

6. Protect data and devices

  • Do not publish IMEI, serial numbers, account identifiers, private notifications or location metadata.
  • Do not dismantle devices or perform unsafe battery or durability experiments.
  • Stop testing if a device shows a safety warning, swelling, unusual odour or excessive heat.
  • Follow the manufacturer's safety and handling guidance.

7. Disclose support and preserve independence

Clearly state whether hardware was personally owned, loaned, donated or sponsored. Do not promise positive conclusions. A company may flag factual errors, but the researcher remains responsible for the method, analysis and conclusions.

8. Corrections

If a published report contains a material error, the project should correct it visibly and date the revision. Where possible, preserve a note explaining what changed.

Important limitation: DeviceLab India is an early-stage independent project. It is not claiming laboratory accreditation, university affiliation, formal certification or manufacturer endorsement.

Before publishing a report

  • Verify formulas and units
  • Check every chart against raw data
  • Remove private device identifiers
  • Disclose device support
  • List limitations
  • Keep a dated version
